# Hand-annotated review of `main.pdf` — 2026-09-26

The author annotated the 13-page PDF of 2026-09-25 on a tablet (30 ink marks over pages
1–11). The paper was refreshed to 1217 fixtures and gained srcDiff as an eighth tool between that
PDF and this pass, so every mark was applied to the refreshed text; where a mark's sentence had
already changed, the row says how. The annotated PDF lives outside the repository; the marks
are transcribed here so the list stands on its own.

**STATUS: every mark is applied and the paper builds at 14 pages (13 before; the two new authors
take the extra space). V1, V2 and V3 below are resolved.**

Three marks turned out to be about claims that were wrong, not about wording:

* **R19** asked what happens to N:M nodes in the line-based comparison. The paragraph said they are
  excluded from every tool's evaluation. They are not: `benchmark_other` projects each multi-map
  group through one representative pairing (`human_mapping::representative_entries`, members
  paired in source order), and only OmniDiff's node-level accuracy grades a group as correct under
  any of its pairings (`check_group_entry`). The paragraph now says that.
* **R24** asked for the tree beside Figure 5. Drawing it showed the caption and prose were wrong:
  tree-sitter-python parses the before snippet into **one** `boolean_operator`, not two nested
  `binary_expression`s, and the after snippet into two, not three. The example still makes its
  point - either after node can be matched at the same cost and the same rendering - and the
  caption, prose and trees now describe the real ASTs (checked with `ascii_visualizer`).
* **R12** asked to double-check that `diff` uses Myers. It does (gnulib `diffseq.h` names Myers
  1986), but unless `--minimal` is given it applies Eggert's `TOO_EXPENSIVE` heuristic, so the
  script is not always the shortest one. Section 2 says so now, and the introduction's "a shortest
  edit script" became "an edit script".
