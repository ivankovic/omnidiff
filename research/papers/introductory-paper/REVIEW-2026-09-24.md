## F4 revisited, the same day: why diff quality matters, from the literature

Restoring the RQ groups (R9) reopened the point behind the second author's F4: the introduction
asserted that diff quality shapes review and never said why. The author's answer was to expand
outward rather than restructure, so the introduction now carries two evidence paragraphs between
its opening and the line-based-diff paragraph, and `references.bib` gained seventeen entries
(metadata from doi.org/Crossref, not transcribed). Three strands:

| strand | evidence cited |
|---|---|
| understanding the change is the work of review | Bacchelli & Bird 2013 (Microsoft: understanding is the main challenge); Tao et al. 2012 (engineers ask for structure, not text deltas) |
| presentation alone moves review outcomes | Fregnan et al. 2022 and Rahman et al. 2026 (file position: 64% lower odds of finding a defect in the last file); Alcocer et al. 2023 (unified vs. split view, eye-tracking); Herzig & Zeller 2013, Barnett et al. 2015, di Biase et al. 2019 (tangled changes and decomposition, controlled experiment); Baum et al. 2019 and Gonçalves et al. 2022 (working memory predicts finding delocalised defects; cognitive load) |
| time and quality, and the diffs that show moves | Sadowski et al. 2018 (Google), Kononenko et al. 2016 (Mozilla), McIntosh et al. 2016 (review participation and defects), Kudrjavets & Rastogi 2023 (speed matters), Meta 2022 (satisfaction falls with P75 review time); Ge et al. 2017 and Brito & Valente 2021 (refactoring-aware diffs lower cognitive effort); Decker et al. 2020 (srcDiff's deltas judged more understandable than the cheapest tree edit) |

The paragraphs close on the gap those studies leave: none of them varies the correspondence the
diff draws, which is what the four RQ groups measure. Three of the seventeen were read from
abstracts or citations only (Alcocer 2023, Rahman 2026, Ge 2017); check them against the full
text before submission. The paper is 13 pages with them.

## R15 — why Figure 2 still shows 30% Unknown

The classifier *was* improved: `src/code/tip.rs`'s tables were expanded on 2026-09-13/14
(`b7c4b19a`), and its own doc comment recorded the dry-run effect over the Full-list database as
29.3% unclassified → 6.6%. What never happened is writing that back: the `tip` column of
`/var/tmp/research/full/stats.sqlite` is what `file_stats` stored at measurement time
(2026-09-07), and the only run since (2026-09-20) re-processed the files above 1 MiB and nothing
else. `plots/tips.png` is rendered from that column, so it still shows the 2026-09-07
classification.

`make reclassify-tips MODE=full` (dry run, 2026-09-24) over the 7,045,754 rows:

| category | before | | after | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Code | 3,890,994 | 55.22% | 4,688,419 | 66.54% |
| Configuration | 84,973 | 1.21% | 176,475 | 2.50% |
| Data | 911,023 | 12.93% | 1,593,317 | 22.61% |
| Documentation | 45,880 | 0.65% | 153,225 | 2.17% |
| Unknown | 2,112,884 | 29.99% | 434,318 | 6.16% |

1,732,406 rows move, 770,380 of them from Unknown to Code. That last number is why the write-back
is not the whole fix: a file the classifier now calls Code was never read, so it has no bytes,
lines or node count, and Figure 3's code-file distributions (n = 3,890,994 today) would either
count it as unmeasured or silently change their population. The consistent fix is
`make measure-file-stats MODE=full`, which re-walks the checkouts with the current tables and
measures what it classifies, then `make introductory-paper-empirical MODE=full`.

**Run the same evening.** The re-walk went 18:01–20:54 (2h53m, 7,444 repositories, one
`file_stats` process each, no aborts), then the report. Figure 2 now shows Unknown at 6.2% and
Code at 66.5%, and every empirical macro moved with it; `research/data/corpus_stats/PROVENANCE.md`
("Re-measured with the 2026-09-13 classifier, 2026-09-24") records the run and what changed,
including the 820,078 newly classified code files that have no grammar. Those would have counted
as zero-node files in Figure 3 and pulled the node median down; on the author's decision the
node distribution and percentiles are now over the code files that parse, with their own `n`.
